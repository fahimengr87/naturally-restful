Melatonin is the most-purchased sleep supplement in the world, and most people are taking it wrong in a specific, predictable, fixable way. Not dangerously wrong — just wastefully. Doses 5–10x larger than the research supports, at times that don't match the hormone's actual job description.

After reading the dose-finding literature, I'm confident in this claim: if you're taking more than 1 mg, you're paying for side effects, not sleep.

## What melatonin does — and what it doesn't

Understand this one point and everything else about melatonin makes sense: **melatonin is a clock signal, not a sedative.** Your pineal gland releases it in the evening to tell your brain "night is starting." It shifts the *timing* of your sleep — it doesn't force sleep to happen.

This is precisely why melatonin works brilliantly for jet lag and shift work (timing problems) and disappoints for everyday stress insomnia (an arousal problem). If your body clock is in the wrong timezone, melatonin is the correct tool. If your mind is replaying an awkward conversation from three years ago, melatonin won't touch that. Different problem, different solution.

## The dose problem — and it's costing you money

Jet-lag and shift-work trials consistently show benefits at **0.5–1 mg**. Above 1 mg, sleep-timing effects plateau — additional melatonin doesn't shift your clock further. What it does add is next-morning grogginess.

Read that carefully: **more melatonin doesn't work better. It just adds side effects.**

Now look at your bottle.

It says 5 mg or 10 mg, doesn't it? Here's why: US melatonin is sold as a dietary supplement, not a drug. There's no dose cap. Brands compete on shelf presence — "10 mg" looks more serious than "1 mg" to someone scanning the aisle. It's marketing, not medicine.

And the situation is worse than just the label. Independent lab analyses of melatonin products have found actual content ranging from **−83% to +478% of the claimed dose.** Your "3 mg" gummy might contain 0.5 mg or 14 mg. This is a quality-control crisis in this category, and it's why I recommend tablets from third-party-tested brands — not gummies, which have the worst quality-control record.

## Timing: the lever most people ignore

Because melatonin is a clock signal, *when* you take it matters more than how much. This table is more useful than anything on the bottle:

| Your situation | Exactly what to do |
|---|---|
| Can't fall asleep until 2 a.m. | 0.5–1 mg, **3–4 hours before** your target bedtime. Yes, that early. It shifts the clock forward; it doesn't knock you out. |
| Flying east across 5+ time zones | 0.5–1 mg at local bedtime at destination, starting the evening *before* you fly. Continue 3–4 nights. |
| Night-shift work | Get medical guidance — the timing protocol is genuinely complex and worth doing with a sleep specialist. |
| Stress insomnia, mind racing | Melatonin is the wrong tool. Try [magnesium glycinate](/articles/magnesium-glycinate-vs-citrate-for-sleep) or [L-theanine](/articles/l-theanine-for-a-racing-mind) instead. |

The most common mistake I see: popping 10 mg at midnight after lying awake for an hour. That's too much, too late, for the wrong problem. Three errors in one swallow.

## Safety — brief but non-negotiable

- **Short-term adult use at correct doses is well established as safe.** This is one of the most-studied sleep supplements.
- **Children:** growing use, but this needs a pediatrician's guidance — not a gummy brand's Instagram ad.
- **Interactions:** warfarin (blood thinner) is the most important. Also immunosuppressants, diabetes medication, and contraceptives. If any apply, ask a pharmacist — literally two minutes.
- **Pregnancy/nursing:** insufficient data. Skip it.

## What to do instead (if melatonin isn't your answer)

For stress-related sleep trouble: [magnesium glycinate](/articles/magnesium-glycinate-vs-citrate-for-sleep) and [ashwagandha](/articles/ashwagandha-for-stress-what-the-research-says) have stronger evidence for this specific problem. For a racing mind: [L-theanine](/articles/l-theanine-for-a-racing-mind) targets exactly that.

Melatonin is a precision timing tool. Use it for timing problems. Use the right tool for the actual problem you have.
