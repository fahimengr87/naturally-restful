Shift work is a fight against your own circadian architecture — a fight biology usually wins. The supplement playbook that follows is built around one principle: **you're not treating insomnia, you're managing a clock problem.** Different job, different tools.

## The one with real evidence: melatonin, used as a clock-shifter

The research on shift workers supports **low-dose melatonin (0.5–1 mg) taken before daytime sleep** — not as a sedative, but as a darkness signal that helps the clock accept sleep at an unnatural hour. Key details:

- **Low dose beats high dose** — [the melatonin dosing trap](/articles/melatonin-most-people-take-too-much) applies double on shift schedules
- **Timing matters more than dose** — 30–60 min before your post-shift sleep block
- **Blackout room is non-negotiable** — the hormone whispers; daylight screams

## Strategic caffeine: front-load it

Caffeine has a ~5–6 hour tail. For night shifts: **dose at shift start, hard stop by mid-shift** (roughly 6+ hours before planned sleep). Pairing it with [L-theanine](/articles/l-theanine-for-a-racing-mind) smooths jitters without adding tail-time.

## For the wired-after-shift problem

Coming off a 3 a.m. shift with a racing brain is the shift worker's signature complaint. The toolkit: dim light on the commute home (sunglasses count), a wind-down ritual, and if supplementing — [glycine](/articles/glycine-for-sleep-the-3-gram-dose) or [magnesium glycinate](/articles/magnesium-before-bed-timing-and-dosage) at the "new bedtime," with realistic expectations.

## What doesn't transfer

Most "sleep supplement" advice assumes a normal clock. Ashwagandha's morning cortisol rhythm logic blurs on rotating schedules; adaptogen [timing debates](/articles/rhodiola-vs-ashwagandha-for-burnout) assume day-night structure. Keep shift stacks minimal: melatonin + caffeine discipline + light management outrank everything else in the research.
