Here's a detail the front of every ashwagandha bottle hides: **"ashwagandha" is not one substance**, and the impressive trials you've heard about were almost all run on one of two patented, standardized extracts — **KSM-66** or **Sensoril**. A jar of undefined root powder has little to do with either.

If you're going to try ashwagandha, this is the article that tells you what the label should say.

## KSM-66: the root-only workhorse

- **What it is:** a standardized root-only extract at 5% withanolides (the active steroidal lactones).
- **The research:** the deepest pool. The 2012 Chandrasekhar trial on chronically stressed adults (300 mg twice daily, ~28% average cortisol reduction), follow-up stress and anxiety trials, and the 2019 Salve sleep study (300 mg twice daily, improved sleep onset and quality) all used KSM-66. Sports-science work on recovery and testosterone also leans on it.
- **Character:** the conservative, tradition-aligned option — ayurvedic practice uses the root, and KSM-66 keeps it that way.

## Sensoril: the concentrated blend

- **What it is:** an extract from **root and leaves**, standardized higher — around 10% withanolides.
- **The research:** fewer but solid trials, notably on stress and mental cognition (the Auddy 2008 work used 125–250 mg and reported cortisol reductions in the mid-teens to high-20s percent, plus improvements in fatigue and concentration).
- **Character:** more concentrated per milligram, which is why Sensoril doses run lower (125–250 mg vs KSM-66's 300–600 mg). The trade: it includes leaf, which traditional use mostly avoided.

## Does the difference matter in practice?

Honestly: probably less than marketing suggests. Both have human trials showing stress and cortisol effects in the same direction. If you want the extract with the most replications — especially for **sleep and stress** — KSM-66 has the deeper file. If a product you already trust uses Sensoril, the evidence doesn't argue against it.

What matters far more than the brand duel:

1. **Standardization at all.** "Ashwagandha root powder 500 mg" with no withanolide percentage is the real gamble — potency varies wildly between raw batches.
2. **Third-party testing.** Withanolide content in unverified products drifts badly from labels.
3. **The exclusions don't change:** pregnancy, thyroid conditions, autoimmune disease — see the [full ashwagandha research article](/articles/ashwagandha-for-stress-what-the-research-says).
