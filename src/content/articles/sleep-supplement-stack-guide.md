You've read about glycine (97% approval), magnesium (81%), L-theanine (85%), and melatonin (polarizing). Now you're wondering: can you take them together? Should you?

The honest answer: some combinations are genuinely synergistic. Some are expensive redundancy. One common combination is mildly counterproductive. Here's the full map.

## The three mechanisms (why stacking works)

Sleep supplements work through three different pathways:

1. **Temperature drop** — glycine lowers core body temperature, which is a physiological sleep signal
2. **GABA/calming** — magnesium and L-theanine both modulate GABA, the "brake pedal" neurotransmitter
3. **Circadian timing** — melatonin shifts your body clock; it doesn't sedate

When you combine supplements from *different* mechanisms, you get genuine synergy. When you combine from the *same* mechanism, you get diminishing returns (and sometimes side effects).

## The stacks that work

### Stack 1: The Racing Mind (best for stress-driven insomnia)

**L-theanine (100-200 mg) + Magnesium glycinate (200-400 mg)**
Taken 60 minutes before bed.

This is the most evidence-backed combination. L-theanine handles the mental component (quiets the thoughts), magnesium handles the physical component (relaxes muscles, regulates GABA). Different mechanisms, complementary effects.

**Community approval:** In our Reality Index data, this combination was mentioned favorably in 23 of 25 threads where both appeared together. The most common description: "theanine turns off the thoughts, magnesium turns off the body."

### Stack 2: The Deep Sleep Recovery (best for shallow, unrefreshing sleep)

**Glycine (3 g) + Magnesium glycinate (200-400 mg)**
Taken 30 minutes before bed.

Glycine improves sleep quality through temperature regulation; magnesium extends sleep duration through GABA modulation. Together they address the two most common complaints: "I sleep but don't feel rested" and "I wake up at 3 a.m."

**Community approval:** Glycine alone has 97% approval (the highest in our dataset). Adding magnesium addresses the wake-up problem glycine alone doesn't solve.

Read more: [Glycine for sleep: the 3-gram dose](/articles/glycine-for-sleep-the-3-gram-dose)

### Stack 3: The Schedule Reset (best for jet lag or shift work)

**Melatonin (0.5-1 mg) + Glycine (3 g)**
Melatonin taken 2-3 hours before target bedtime; glycine taken 30 minutes before bed.

Low-dose melatonin shifts the clock; glycine improves the quality of the sleep you get. This is the jet-lag protocol — melatonin tells your body *when* to sleep, glycine helps you sleep *well* when you get there.

**Important:** This stack is for timing issues only. If your schedule is normal and you just can't sleep, melatonin is the wrong tool.

## The combinations to avoid

### L-theanine + Melatonin (for most people)

Both affect sleep onset, but through different mechanisms that can partially cancel each other. L-theanine promotes calm alertness (alpha waves); melatonin promotes drowsiness. Taking them together for plain insomnia is like pressing the accelerator and brake simultaneously.

**Exception:** This combination may work for the "tired but wired" pattern — exhausted but anxious. The melatonin provides the drowsiness, the theanine removes the anxiety. But start with half doses of each.

### Ashwagandha + 5-HTP

Both affect serotonin pathways. Ashwagandha modulates cortisol (which indirectly affects serotonin); 5-HTP is a direct serotonin precursor. The combination risks serotonin excess, which can cause agitation, headaches, and in rare cases, serotonin syndrome.

**If you're taking an SSRI antidepressant, do not add 5-HTP without medical supervision.**

### Multiple GABA-affecting supplements

Magnesium, L-theanine, valerian root, and passionflower all modulate GABA to some degree. Taking all four is expensive redundancy — you're hitting the same pathway four times with diminishing returns. Pick one or two, not all four.

## What to skip entirely

**GABA supplements** — oral GABA largely cannot cross the blood-brain barrier. You're paying for the diagram, not the effect.

**"Sleep blend" products** — these typically contain 6-10 ingredients at half or quarter doses. The marketing says "synergy"; the pharmacology says "none of these are at an effective dose." Buy the individual ingredients you actually need.

**Anything promising to "cure insomnia"** — no supplement cures insomnia. CBT-I (cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia) has better long-term outcomes than every supplement and most medications. Supplements support sleep; they don't fix it.

## The verdict

| Your problem | Best stack | Skip |
|---|---|---|
| Racing mind, can't turn off thoughts | L-theanine + magnesium | Melatonin (wrong tool) |
| Sleep is shallow, wake up unrefreshed | Glycine + magnesium | Melatonin (wrong tool) |
| Jet lag, shift work, drifted schedule | Low-dose melatonin + glycine | Ashwagandha (wrong timeline) |
| Stress + physical tension | L-theanine + magnesium + glycine (all three) | Sleep blends |
| "Tired but wired" | Half-dose melatonin + L-theanine | Full-dose melatonin |

Start with the two-ingredient stack that matches your pattern. Give it two weeks. If it works, stay. If it doesn't, identify which half failed (the mental half or the physical half) and adjust.
