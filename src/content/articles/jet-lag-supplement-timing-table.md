Jet lag is the one sleep problem where melatonin genuinely shines — because jet lag **is** a clock-timing problem, and [melatonin is a clock signal](/articles/melatonin-most-people-take-too-much), not a sedative. The catch: most people take the wrong dose at the wrong time. Here's the table.

## The timing table

| Flight | Melatonin plan | Light plan |
|---|---|---|
| **Eastbound** (e.g., BD→US, EU→Asia... you lose hours) | 0.5–1 mg at **local bedtime, destination time**, starting the evening *before* travel if possible; continue 3–4 evenings | Morning light at destination — critical; avoid evening light |
| **Westbound** (you gain hours) | Usually **skip melatonin**; you need to stay *up*, not sleep earlier | Evening light; morning darkness if waking too early |
| **More than 8 zones crossed** | Treat as eastbound for the first days, then adjust | Daylight discipline both ends |

## The 48-hour frequent-flyer protocol

1. **Shift caffeine:** morning-only at destination; none after local noon
2. **Anchor meal times to the destination clock immediately** — meals are secondary clock cues
3. **First destination night:** the low-dose melatonin above + blackout room; do not "just nap" through the local afternoon — a 90-minute nap max, before 3 p.m.
4. **Days 2–3:** morning light exposure, melatonin only if still drifting late

## What doesn't work

- **High-dose melatonin (5–10 mg) for jet lag** — more grogginess, zero extra clock-shift benefit
- **"Jet lag pills" blends** — usually melatonin + caffeine + vitamins priced like perfume
- **Alcohol on the plane** — the dehydration and rebound wakefulness cost more than any pill repays

## Bottom line

Rule of thumb: **east = melatonin at local bedtime + morning light; west = light and discipline, skip the pill.** Small dose, right clock, and jet lag shrinks from a week of misery to a couple of groggy mornings.
