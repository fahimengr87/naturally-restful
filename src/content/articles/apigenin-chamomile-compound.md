Every few years, one supplement gets the podcast treatment and shelves empty out. This cycle it's apigenin — a flavonoid from chamomile, promoted as a natural sleep aid that "activates GABA." Here's the honest file.

## Where the claims come from

- **Chamomile itself** has mild human evidence for sleep quality and anxiety — gentle, modest, real (mostly in randomized trials of chamomile *tea*).
- **Isolated apigenin** has a much thinner file: mostly **animal and cell studies** showing sedative-adjacent effects via GABA-A receptors — the same neighborhood benzodiazepines act in, though far more weakly.
- **Human sleep trials of isolated apigenin: essentially absent.** Dosing recommendations circulating online (50 mg) are extrapolated, not established.

## The honest framing

Apigenin isn't fake — it's *early*. The mechanism is plausible, chamomile's track record lends soft support, and early is exactly when honest reviewers say "unproven" rather than "miracle." Compare with [glycine](/articles/glycine-for-sleep-the-3-gram-dose) or [magnesium](/articles/magnesium-glycinate-vs-citrate-for-sleep), where human trials actually exist, and you see the difference between a hypothesis and a track record.

## If you want to try it anyway

Reasonable rules: 50 mg in the evening, from a third-party tested brand, for 2–4 weeks, expecting subtle-or-nothing. One caution — because apigenin touches the same receptor family as anti-anxiety medication, anyone on benzodiazepines or sedatives should skip it or ask a pharmacist first.

## Bottom line

Drink the chamomile — pleasant, mildly supported, zero risk. Treat isolated apigenin as the experiment it is, not the answer podcasts pretend. Our [comparison page](/best-sleep-supplements) ranks it "promising but unproven," and that's the whole story until human data arrives.
