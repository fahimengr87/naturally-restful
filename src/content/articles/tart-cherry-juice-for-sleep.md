Tart cherry juice occupies a rare position: a *food* with multiple randomized sleep trials behind it. Not as many as melatonin, far fewer than magnesium — but for a whole-fruit intervention, the evidence file is genuinely interesting. Here's the honest read.

## What the trials found

- **Howatson et al. (2012)** gave older adults with insomnia 240 ml of Montmorency cherry juice twice daily for a week: modestly improved sleep time and efficiency versus placebo, with urinary melatonin modestly increased — the juice contains a little natural melatonin and its precursor tryptophan.
- **Losso et al. (2018)** in adults with insomnia reported increased sleep time (around 84 minutes in their small sample) — an eye-catching number from a small trial that hasn't been replicated at scale. Treat it as the optimistic end of the range, not the expectation.
- Supporting angles: cherry juice also carries anti-inflammatory anthocyanins, and exercise-recovery studies show small sleep-quality benefits in athletes.

**The honest average:** small samples, subjective endpoints, industry-adjacent funding in several cases. Real signal, modest size, unproven at scale — the classic "promising food" profile, not a treatment.

## The sugar catch nobody mentions

The trial protocol is 240 ml **twice daily** — roughly 30–50 g of sugar per day depending on the brand, taken at breakfast *and before bed*. A bedtime sugar hit is an odd trade for a sleep aid. Two mitigations:

- **Use the concentrate** (30 ml diluted in water) — the trial-relevant compounds in less volume
- **Or capsules** of tart cherry extract — zero sugar, though extract dosing is less directly studied than juice

## Who should skip it

- Anyone managing blood sugar (the twice-daily juice protocol is a real glycemic load)
- People on warfarin — cherry's anthocyanin content puts it on some interaction watchlists; ask your pharmacist
- Anyone expecting melatonin-magnitude effects — this is a gentle nudge, not a lever

## Bottom line

Tart cherry is a legitimate "small help, low harm" option — appealing if you prefer food-format interventions and accept small-trial evidence. For targeted supplement-strength effects, [magnesium](/articles/magnesium-before-bed-timing-and-dosage) and [glycine](/articles/glycine-for-sleep-the-3-gram-dose) carry better evidence per dollar, and the [master comparison](/best-sleep-supplements) places cherry in the "promising food, minor league" tier honestly.

**Key studies:** Howatson et al., *Eur J Nutr* 2012 (elderly insomnia, juice ↑ sleep time/efficiency) · Losso et al., *Am J Ther* 2018 (insomnia adults, ↑ sleep ~84 min, small sample) · Ataie-Jafari et al. meta-analysis 2024 (modest pooled improvements, high heterogeneity).
