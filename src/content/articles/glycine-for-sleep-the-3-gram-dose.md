Here's a supplement story that rarely gets told straight: glycine has some of the cleanest sleep trial data of any amino acid — and most products sold as "glycine sleep aids" contain a fraction of the dose those trials used.

This article covers what the research actually tested, what it found, and the practical details nobody mentions (like why powder beats capsules here).

## What the trials did

The glycine sleep research comes mostly from Japanese university groups, and their protocol was consistent across studies: **3 grams of glycine, taken shortly before bed.**

Inagawa and colleagues gave volunteers 3 g before bedtime and found improvements in subjective sleep quality — falling asleep faster, waking less groggy — with better next-day performance on cognitive tasks. Yamadera's team, using polysomnography (the wired-up lab measurement), observed shortened sleep latency. A later trial by Bannai and colleagues found the same pattern, plus an interesting detail: participants reported less daytime sleepiness the following day — meaning better sleep, not drugged sleep.

Now the honest caveats, same as always: these were small trials (typically 10–20 participants per arm), subjective outcomes carried a lot of the weight, and effect sizes were moderate. Glycine is not a knockout pill. It's a "noticeably nicer night" supplement for many people, at low cost and low risk.

## Why it probably works

Two mechanisms come up in the literature:

1. **Body temperature drop.** Glycine triggers peripheral vasodilation — blood flows to your skin, hands and feet warm slightly, and your core temperature falls. That core temperature drop is one of the body's natural "initiate sleep" signals; it's the same reason a warm bath before bed paradoxically helps you sleep.
2. **Direct inhibitory signaling.** Glycine is itself an inhibitory neurotransmitter — one of the brain's "quiet down" molecules — though how much orally taken glycine crosses into the brain is still debated.

## How to actually take it

- **Dose: 3 g.** This is the trial dose. Many "sleep blend" capsules contain 500 mg or less — you'd need six of them.
- **Powder, not capsules.** One small scoop of glycine powder in water. It tastes mildly sweet (it's the sweetest-tasting amino acid), dissolves instantly, and there's no pill fatigue.
- **Timing: 30–60 minutes before bed.**
- **Duration:** effects on sleep onset were often reported within days; give it two weeks before judging.

Glycine also stacks naturally with magnesium glycinate — which is literally magnesium bound to glycine — though the glycine dose from that route is far below 3 g. If you take both, you're not double-dosing anything dangerous; they're just modestly complementary.

## Who should skip it

- **Anyone on clozapine.** A documented, if rare, interaction — talk to your psychiatrist first.
- **People with a history of schizophrenia or psychosis.** Glycine interacts with NMDA receptor systems that are relevant to these conditions; high-dose glycine was actually studied *as* a psychiatric intervention. Not a DIY supplement in this group.
- **Pregnancy/nursing:** insufficient data, as with most supplements. Skip.
