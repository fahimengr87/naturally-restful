Ask the internet about magnesium deficiency and you'll get a list of twenty symptoms that describe nearly everyone's tired Tuesday. The reality is more specific — and more interesting: **true deficiency is rarer than supplement marketing claims, but insufficiency is common, and blood tests are bad at catching it.**

## The symptoms with actual signal

Early magnesium insufficiency is mostly neurological and muscular:

- **Muscle twitches and cramps** — the classic, though cramps have many causes
- **Eyelid flutter** (fasciculations) — a frequently reported early sign
- **Heightened stress response and irritability** — magnesium calms NMDA/excitatory signaling; low levels amplify the startle reflex
- **Poor sleep quality** — the link that brings most readers to [our magnesium guides](/articles/magnesium-before-bed-timing-and-dosage)
- In worse cases: numbness, tingling, and (severely low, usually with other illnesses) heart rhythm changes — that's hospital territory, not supplement territory

The pattern to notice: symptoms of a nervous system stuck slightly on "high."

## Why your blood test says "normal"

Only about 1% of body magnesium sits in blood serum — the rest is in bone and cells. Serum magnesium is defended tightly and drops late. A "normal" serum level therefore says little about your tissue levels. (Research settings use more sensitive markers; routine clinics usually don't.)

## Who's genuinely at risk

- People with **GI conditions** (Crohn's, celiac, chronic diarrhea) — poor absorption
- **Type 2 diabetes** — urinary losses
- **Heavy alcohol use** — the classic double hit (losses + poor intake)
- **Older adults** — intake and absorption both decline
- **PPI (acid-reflex medication) long-term users** — a well-documented, under-appreciated cause
- Anyone eating mostly processed food — processing strips magnesium

If none apply and you eat whole grains, nuts, seeds, and greens daily, you're likely fine — and no supplement on earth will "boost" you beyond normal.

## Bottom line

Low magnesium is real but oversold. If the symptom pattern fits *and* a risk factor fits, a modest glycinate trial (200–400 mg, 2–4 weeks, [how to do it properly](/articles/magnesium-before-bed-timing-and-dosage)) is reasonable — ideally with your doctor's awareness, since symptoms like cramps and fatigue have more serious mimics that deserve ruling out first.
