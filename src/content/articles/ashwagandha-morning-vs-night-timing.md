The internet has decided ashwagandha timing is a personality test. Morning people swear it goes with coffee. Night people swear it goes with brushing your teeth. Both cite studies neither has read.

Here's what the actual trials say, in plain words.

## The short answer

**Timing matters less than consistency.** Every major trial that made ashwagandha famous — the Chandrasekhar 2012 KSM-66 study, the 2019 stress-and-sleep trial, the anxiety reductions — gave it at roughly the same time every day. That's the variable that mattered. Morning versus night was barely a footnote.

The famous 2012 trial that cut cortisol ~28%? Evening dosing. The sleep-specific trials? Also evening. But the anxiety and energy trials? Morning, sometimes split doses. The pattern isn't "one true time" — it's "whatever time you'll actually remember tomorrow."

## The one difference that's real

Ashwagandha makes some people drowsy in the first week. Not sedated — just a mild heaviness as your body adjusts to lower cortisol. For those people, morning dosing means a groggy Tuesday.

The fix is simple: **if your first week on it makes you sleepy, take it at night.** If it makes you feel nothing — the majority experience — take it whenever you'll remember it. The drowsiness fades by week two regardless of timing.

## The case for night (if you're using it for sleep)

Ashwagandha's sleep benefits come from cortisol reduction — and cortisol follows a daily curve, peaking in the morning and dipping at night. Taking it 1–2 hours before bed aligns with the dip. This is also how the sleep trials dosed it.

**The night protocol:** 300–600 mg of a standardized extract (KSM-66 or Sensoril), 1–2 hours before bed, every night for 6–8 weeks. That's the trial recipe. Not "when you remember."

## The case for morning (if you're using it for stress or energy)

If daytime stress is your problem — racing mind at 2 PM, wired by evening — morning dosing gives the cortisol reduction a head start on your worst hours. The anxiety trials used morning or split dosing for exactly this reason.

**The morning protocol:** same dose, same extract, with breakfast. Skip the "take it sublingually on an empty stomach at 5 AM" advice from forums — there's no trial behind that, only vibes.

## What doesn't matter (that people argue about)

- **Empty stomach vs. with food.** No meaningful difference in the trial data. Take it however your stomach prefers.
- **Cycling timing throughout the week.** There's no evidence your body "needs variety." Consistency won.
- **Taking it "right when cortisol peaks."** Your cortisol peaks within 30–45 minutes of waking. Unless you're setting a 6 AM alarm for a capsule, this optimization is a fantasy.

## The verdict

**For sleep: night, 1–2 hours before bed. For daytime stress: morning. For everyone else: the time you'll actually stick to for 8 weeks, because consistency is the active ingredient the trials measured.**
