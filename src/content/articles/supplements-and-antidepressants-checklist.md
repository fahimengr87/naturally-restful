Important framing first: this page is **information, not medical advice**, and anyone on psychiatric medication should run every supplement past their prescriber or pharmacist — a two-minute conversation that prevents real harm. That said, the interaction landscape has known shape. Here's the map.

## The genuinely risky tier

- **St. John's Wort** — the notorious one: a potent serotonin syndrome risk with SSRIs/SNRIs/MAOIs, plus it *lowers blood levels* of many other drugs via liver enzyme induction. Never combine. (We don't recommend it at all.)
- **5-HTP and tryptophan** — serotonin precursors; combining with serotonergic antidepressants stacks toward serotonin syndrome risk. Prescriber conversation mandatory.
- **High-dose SAMe** — same serotonergic stacking concern.

## The caution tier (dose-dependent, prescriber-dependent)

- **Ashwagandha** — thyroid interaction (relevant if on thyroid augmentation), possible sedative additivity, immune stimulation (relevant alongside immune-modulating treatments). Our [full exclusion list](/articles/ashwagandha-side-effects-honest-list) applies with extra force here.
- **Valerian** — sedative additivity with sedating psychiatric meds — [the valerian file](/articles/valerian-root-honest-review).
- **Melatonin** — mixed, mostly small interactions; some prescribers are comfortable, others not. Ask.

## The generally-lower-risk tier for sleep support

- **Magnesium (glycinate)** — no direct serotonergic activity; the main caution is general (kidney disease, antibiotic spacing)
- **Glycine** — a food-level amino acid; no established antidepressant interaction
- **L-theanine** — minimal interaction signal, though BP-medication users should note its mild BP effect

"Generally lower risk" is not "zero risk with your specific regimen" — the two-minute pharmacist check remains the rule.

## Why this page exists

Most supplement sites bury interactions; a site that [leads with them](/articles/ashwagandha-side-effects-honest-list) is either foolish or honest — we choose honest, because readers on psychiatric treatment deserve a map with the minefields drawn in ink.
