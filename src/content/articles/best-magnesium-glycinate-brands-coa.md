Every magnesium bottle promises the same thing. The difference between an honest brand and a label-printer is one document most shoppers never ask for: the **Certificate of Analysis (COA)**. This short guide makes you the rare buyer who asks.

## What a COA is

A COA is a lab report for a specific production batch. It verifies what's actually in the jar: elemental magnesium content, the glycinate form, and — more importantly — what's *not* in it (heavy metals, contaminants). Reputable brands either publish COAs on their website or email them on request. Silence is an answer.

## The four things to check

1. **Elemental magnesium per serving** — matches the label claim (within normal analytical variance). Products that "accidentally" overstate content are common in this category.
2. **Heavy metals: lead, arsenic, cadmium, mercury** — present in trace amounts in everything; the COA should show them *below* limits (California Prop 65 or USP standards are common references).
3. **The form** — genuine glycinate/bisglycinate, not "magnesium oxide blended with a little glycine" dressed up as "chelated." The ingredient list should say bisglycinate or glycinate chelate, not oxide.
4. **Batch and date** — a COA for *your* batch, or at least a recent one. A 3-year-old document proves nothing about what's on shelves now.

## Third-party marks that shortcut the work

Don't want to read lab documents? Look for these on the label — they represent independent verification programs:

- **NSF Certified for Sport** — the strictest
- **USP Verified** — gold standard for content accuracy
- **Informed Choice / Informed Sport** — common for sports products

A brand with none of these *and* no published COAs is asking you to trust its printer.
