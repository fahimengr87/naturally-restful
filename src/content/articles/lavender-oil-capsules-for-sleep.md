Most people meet lavender as a scent — pillow sprays, candles, the aromatherapy aisle. Far fewer know that **swallowed lavender oil**, as the standardized extract Silexan, has one of the better clinical files in the herbal calm category. The distinction between smelling and swallowing matters, because the evidence lives almost entirely on the swallow side.

## What the trials show

- **Generalized anxiety:** multiple randomized trials (Woelk & Schlafke 2010; Kasper et al. 2014) found Silexan 80–160 mg/day significantly reduced anxiety scores versus placebo — effects in the same neighborhood as some pharmaceutical comparators in those studies, without sedation.
- **Sleep:** anxiety trials consistently noted **improved sleep quality** as a secondary outcome — lavender's sleep benefit appears to travel through its anxiety reduction.
- **Aromatherapy lavender** (the smell) has weaker, mostly small studies — pleasant, but a different and smaller evidence class than the capsules.

## Practical notes

- **Dose in trials:** 80–160 mg/day of Silexan specifically — not "lavender essential oil off the shelf," which is not for swallowing in random quantities. Standardized capsule or nothing.
- **Onset:** weeks, like most regulatory-style interventions — this is gardening, not a switch.
- **Cautions:** occasional GI upset; rare allergic dermatitis with lavender generally; theoretical interactions with sedatives — pharmacist check if combined.

## Bottom line

Silexan is one of the best-kept secrets in the calm category: real anxiety trials, sleep benefits as a passenger, non-sedating, and cheap. It earns a legitimate supporting slot in our [comparison](/best-sleep-supplements) for anxiety-first readers.

**Key studies:** Woelk & Schlafke, *Phytomedicine* 2010 (Silexan vs sertraline vs placebo, GAD) · Kasper et al., *Int J Neuropsychopharmacol* 2014 (Silexan 80/160 mg, anxiety + sleep quality) · Kasper et al., *World J Biol Psychiatry* 2015 (pooled safety/tolerability).
