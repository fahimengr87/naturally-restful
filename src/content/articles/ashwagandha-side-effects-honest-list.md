We recommend ashwagandha on this site — [the evidence earns it](/articles/ashwagandha-for-stress-what-the-research-says). But a recommendation without an honest side-effect page is just marketing. This is the other half of the story, and for some readers it's the half that matters.

## The common stuff: mild and rare

In trials at 300–600 mg/day for 8–12 weeks, ashwagandha was well tolerated. What does appear:

- **Mild digestive upset** — stomach discomfort, occasionally loose stools, usually in the first days
- **Drowsiness** — it lowers arousal; stacking it with sedatives compounds this
- **Headaches** — occasionally reported, transient

That's the benign tier. Now the list that actually decides whether you should take it at all.

## The hard exclusions — not "ask your doctor," but "skip it"

1. **Pregnancy: absolute no.** Ashwagandha is associated with miscarriage risk. This is the clearest contraindication in supplements, full stop. Nursing parents: no data, same conclusion.
2. **Thyroid disease without medical supervision.** Ashwagandha pushes thyroid hormone activity upward. For hypothyroid readers it can potentiate medication (doses drift); for hyperthyroidism or Graves' it can make things actively worse. Case reports exist of ashwagandha-triggered thyrotoxicosis. Thyroid condition = doctor's office first, bottle second.
3. **Autoimmune disease or immunosuppressants.** Lupus, rheumatoid arthritis, MS, transplant recipients: ashwagandha stimulates immune activity — the opposite of what immunosuppressive treatment is trying to achieve. Rejection and flare risk are theoretical but serious.

## The interactions tier — pharmacist conversation required

- **Sedatives and anti-anxiety medication** (benzodiazepines, sleep drugs): additive sedation
- **Blood pressure medication:** ashwagandha can lower BP on its own — effects stack
- **Diabetes medication:** it can lower blood glucose additively
- **Thyroid hormone replacement:** see above — monitoring matters

## The two honest uncertainties

- **Liver: rare case reports.** A small number of published case reports link ashwagandha (including branded extracts) to acute liver injury. Reversed after stopping in most cases, but it's a real signal — if you develop dark urine, jaundice, or unexplained fatigue, stop and see a doctor.
- **Long-term use:** trials run 8–12 weeks. Whether 12+ months of continuous use is equally safe is simply not established. Some practitioners recommend 8–12 week cycles with breaks; the science doesn't strongly mandate it, but humility about the unknowns is free.

## Bottom line

For a healthy, non-pregnant adult taking no interacting medications, ashwagandha's risk profile at trial doses is genuinely good — better than most over-the-counter sleep aids. But this page exists because "generally safe" and "safe for you" are different sentences. Walk through the list above honestly before the [first dose](/articles/ashwagandha-vs-l-theanine) — and if two of the tiers touched you, that decision belongs to a clinician, not a website. Even this one.
