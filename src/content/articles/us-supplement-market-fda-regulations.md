Walk into a CVS in Chicago and ask for melatonin. You'll find 10 mg bottles on the shelf. Walk into a pharmacy in Berlin and ask for the same thing. You'll need a prescription — and if you get one, it'll be for 0.5 mg.

Same hormone. Same science. Two completely different regulatory worlds. And understanding why is the difference between wasting money and sleeping well.

## The DSHEA problem (and why it affects your sleep)

In 1994, the US Congress passed the Dietary Supplement Health and Education Act. It classified supplements as **food, not drugs**. This single decision created the supplement market we know today:

- No pre-market safety testing required
- No FDA dose caps
- No requirement to prove the product works at the sold dose
- The FDA can only act AFTER problems appear

Compare this to the European Union, where supplements must be demonstrated safe BEFORE market, doses are capped, and melatonin above 1 mg requires a prescription in Germany, France, and several other countries.

**The result:** American supplement shelves are the Wild West. Products with doses 10-30x the research-supported amount. Proprietary blends that don't disclose quantities. Quality that varies wildly between brands.

## What this means for your sleep supplements

### Melatonin: the dose disaster

The research consistently shows benefits at 0.5–1 mg. American bottles typically contain 5–10 mg. European countries cap supplements at 0.9–1 mg.

You read that correctly: **Europeans are legally limited to the dose the science supports. Americans are sold 10x that amount because no law stops them.**

Our [melatonin dosing guide](/articles/melatonin-most-people-take-too-much) covers the full research. The short version: lower your dose.

### Magnesium: the dose ceiling

The EU sets maximum permitted levels for vitamins and minerals. US supplements have no such caps. American magnesium bottles might contain 500+ mg per serving. EU versions typically cap at 250–375 mg.

The trial doses for sleep — 200–400 mg elemental — fall right in the EU range. American megadoses don't work better; they just cost more and cause more digestive urgency.

### Quality control: the hidden crisis

Independent lab analyses have found US melatonin products containing anywhere from **−83% to +478% of the label claim**. Your "3 mg" gummy might contain 0.5 mg or 14 mg.

This is a US-specific problem. EU products must meet stricter manufacturing standards, and the quality variance is significantly lower.

## How to be a smart American supplement buyer

Three rules that put you ahead of 95% of US consumers:

**1. Look for third-party testing marks.** NSF Certified for Sport, USP Verified, or Informed Choice. These are independent organizations that verify what's actually in the bottle. No mark? You're gambling.

**2. Buy doses that match the research.** 0.5–1 mg melatonin (not 10 mg). 200–400 mg magnesium glycinate (not 500+ mg oxide). The science doesn't change based on what's available at Walgreens.

**3. Check the back panel, not the front.** The front says "Sleep Support Complex." The back reveals magnesium oxide at 4% absorption. The front is marketing; the back is chemistry. Trust the back.

## The bottom line

The US supplement market gives you more choices, higher doses, and lower prices than anywhere else in the world. It also gives you the least consumer protection. The same bottle that's illegal to sell in Germany without a prescription sits freely on American shelves.

That's not a reason to avoid supplements. It's a reason to be a smarter buyer than the system requires you to be. Check certifications, match research doses, and read the back panel. Your sleep — and your wallet — will thank you.
