"Burnout" bundles two different problems into one word: **exhausted days** and **wired nights**. Rhodiola and ashwagandha each solve one of them — and picking wrong means wondering why your adaptogen "isn't working."

## The one-line distinction

- **Rhodiola (Rhodiola rosea)** is the *fatigue* adaptogen: trials show improvements in stress-related exhaustion, mental performance under strain, and morning energy — with effects that can appear within days to two weeks.
- **Ashwagandha** is the *stress-sleep* adaptogen: stronger trial record for anxiety, cortisol reduction (~28% in the key study), and sleep quality — but it builds over 6–8 weeks. [Our full research review is here](/articles/ashwagandha-for-stress-what-the-research-says).

## What the trials actually tested

Rhodiola research centers on the SHR-5 extract in stressed-but-healthy groups: night-shift physicians, students during exams (Spasov 2000), people with stress-related fatigue (Panossian 2010 review; Darbinyan trials). Outcomes: less fatigue, better concentration, improved well-being — modest but consistent, and notably *faster*-acting than ashwagandha.

Ashwagandha's file is deeper for outright stress physiology: cortisol, perceived stress scores, and sleep — which is why it anchors our [supplement comparison](/best-sleep-supplements) rather than rhodiola.

## Match the symptom, not the trend

| Your burnout pattern | Pick | Take |
|---|---|---|
| Dragging all day, foggy, can't start tasks | **Rhodiola** | Morning, with breakfast — it's mildly stimulating |
| Functioning by day but mind races at night | **Ashwagandha** | Evening, daily, expect weeks |
| Classic wired-and-tired (both) | **Both, split** | Rhodiola AM / ashwagandha PM |
| Predominantly anxious, poor sleep | **Ashwagandha** first | See if you still need rhodiola after 8 weeks |

## Practical notes

- **Rhodiola dose:** 200–600 mg/day of a standardized extract (typically 3% rosavins, 1% salidroside). **Take it in the morning** — evening doses can disturb the sleep you're trying to fix.
- **Rhodiola cautions:** its stimulating edge means caution with bipolar disorder and strong stimulants; it can also be mildly activating for sensitive people.
- **Ashwagandha exclusions** (pregnancy, thyroid, autoimmune) haven't changed — [the honest list is here](/articles/ashwagandha-side-effects-honest-list).

## Bottom line

If burnout means your engine won't start, that's rhodiola's job. If it means your brakes won't engage, that's ashwagandha's. And if it's both — as it usually is — the morning/evening split is a well-trodden, sensible combination rather than a gimmick.
