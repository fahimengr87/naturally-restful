Ashwagandha has real trial data behind it. Not the "traditionally used for centuries" kind of data — actual randomized controlled trials with blood measurements and named researchers. That puts it ahead of 95% of the stress supplement aisle.

But the marketing has outrun the evidence, and some of the products on shelves are worthless. Let me separate what's real from what's noise.

## The trial that matters

Chandrasekhar et al., 2012. *Indian Journal of Psychological Medicine.* This is the study everyone quotes, and it deserves the attention:

64 chronically stressed adults. 300 mg of KSM-66 (standardized root extract), twice daily, for 60 days. Here are the numbers:

- **Cortisol dropped 27.9% on average.** Let me be clear about what this means: not a questionnaire score, not "feels calmer" — an actual hormone measured in their blood. A 28% reduction in your primary stress hormone is a significant physiological change.
- Perceived stress scores fell substantially versus placebo
- Sleep quality improved
- Self-reported vitality went up

This wasn't a one-off. Follow-up trials and multiple meta-analyses (Pratte 2014 is the most-cited) point in the same direction: reduced stress and anxiety, measurable cortisol effects, improved sleep. Mostly in stressed-but-healthy adults.

Now the honest limits, because pretending they don't exist would make me just another supplement shill:

**Sample sizes run small.** 40–100 people per trial typically. I'd like to see 500+ replication studies. The direction is consistent, but the precision is modest.

**Publication bias exists.** Positive supplement trials get published; null results disappear. Meta-analyses correct for this and still find real effects. Just appropriately sized ones.

**Extract identity determines everything.** This is where most products fail. "Ashwagandha root powder 500 mg" from a random brand is agricultural material, not a supplement. The trials tested standardized extracts:

- **KSM-66** — root-only, 5% withanolides. The deepest trial file.
- **Sensoril** — root + leaf, ~10% withanolides. Fewer but solid trials at lower doses.
- **Shoden** — newer, 35% withanolides. Early evidence, premium price.

If the label doesn't name one of these extracts and specify the percentage, don't buy it. I'm serious. You're paying for random root dust.

## What you can realistically expect

A **noticeable reduction in perceived stress over 4–8 weeks**. Not life-transformation. Not overnight. The kind of change where someone at work says "you seem less tense lately" and you realize they're right.

Dose: **300–600 mg/day of a standardized extract**, split morning and evening. The trials measured effects at 6–8 weeks. If you're judging after five days, you're wasting your time and money.

## Who must not take this

Three hard stops. I'm not going to soften these:

**Pregnancy: absolutely not.** Ashwagandha is associated with miscarriage. This is the clearest contraindication in the entire supplement category. If you're pregnant, stop reading and close this page.

**Thyroid disease: only with your doctor.** Ashwagandha pushes thyroid hormone activity upward. There are published case reports of ashwagandha-triggered thyrotoxicosis. If you're on thyroid medication or have Graves' disease, this is a conversation for your endocrinologist — not a supplement website.

**Autoimmune disease or immunosuppressants:** ashwagandha stimulates immune activity. That's the opposite of what immunosuppressive treatment does. This combination risks flares and treatment interference.

Additional interactions to flag with a pharmacist: sedatives (additive drowsiness), blood pressure medication (additive lowering), diabetes medication (additive glucose reduction). Two-minute conversation, worth having.

## Bottom line

If your sleep trouble traces back to stress, ashwagandha is the most evidence-backed calm supplement available without a prescription. Buy KSM-66 or Sensoril from a brand that publishes third-party test results. Take the real dose for the real duration.

And if your stress is mild and occasional — a walk and a consistent bedtime will outperform any capsule on this page. I'd be dishonest if I told you otherwise.

**Key studies:** Chandrasekhar et al., *Indian J Psychol Med* 2012 (KSM-66 600 mg/day: cortisol −27.9%) · Salve et al., *Cureus* 2019 (300 mg BID: sleep onset + quality) · Pratte et al., *J Ayurveda Integr Med* 2014 meta-analysis (significant stress/anxiety reduction).
