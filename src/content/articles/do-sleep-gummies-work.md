Sleep gummies took over the aisle for a reason: they feel like candy, taste like candy, and promise pharmaceutical-grade nights. Let's take the promise apart.

## What's actually in them

Reading labels across the category, the standard formulas:

- **Melatonin gummies:** typically 2.5–10 mg. The dose problem is inverted from most products — [research supports 0.5–1 mg](/articles/melatonin-most-people-take-too-much), so many gummies deliver 5–20x the useful amount. One published analysis also found real melatonin content ranged wildly versus labels — from nearly nothing to nearly double.
- **"Calm blend" gummies:** usually magnesium citrate (the laxative form — [why we don't love it for sleep](/articles/magnesium-glycinate-vs-citrate-for-sleep)) plus token L-theanine.
- **Herbal gummies:** valerian/chamomile at doses even the optimistic studies wouldn't recognize ([valerian's real file](/articles/valerian-root-honest-review)).

Plus 2–3 g of sugar per gummy — frequently taken right before bed, the exact timing sugar doesn't belong.

## So do they "work"?

Three honest mechanisms, in order of strength:

1. **The ritual** — a consistent pre-bed cue genuinely conditions sleep readiness. Real effect, worth something.
2. **Placebo with good branding** — sleep outcomes are unusually placebo-responsive; gummies have excellent branding.
3. **The chemistry** — occasionally real (a low-dose melatonin gummy taken at the right *time*), usually overwhelmed by dose chaos.

## The verdict

If you love the ritual: buy the **lowest-dose melatonin gummy you can find (≤1 mg)**, treat it as a timing device, and brush your teeth after. If you want the chemistry to matter: capsules of [glycine](/articles/glycine-for-sleep-the-3-gram-dose), [magnesium glycinate](/articles/best-magnesium-supplements-for-sleep-ranked), or [theanine](/articles/l-theanine-for-a-racing-mind) deliver verified doses without the bedtime sugar.
