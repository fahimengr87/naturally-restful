Magnesium threonate (usually sold as Magtein®) is the most expensive form of magnesium on the shelf — often **5–10x the price per milligram** of glycinate. The premium buys a specific story: "the only magnesium that crosses into your brain." Here's how that story holds up.

## Where the claims come from

The excitement traces to preclinical research from around 2010–2011, where magnesium L-threonate raised brain magnesium levels in **rats** and improved synaptic markers and cognitive performance in aged animals. It's genuinely interesting basic science.

The human evidence is one main randomized trial: a 12-week study in older adults with cognitive complaints, showing modest improvements on some cognitive measures versus placebo. It was small, industry-linked, and the effects were subtle. For **sleep specifically**, human threonate data is thin — a long way from the confident claims on the label.

## The uncomfortable math

Threonate delivers relatively little elemental magnesium per dose — a typical serving (2,000 mg magnesium L-threonate) provides roughly **144 mg of actual magnesium**. Compare glycinate at 200–400 mg elemental for a fraction of the price, with a decent (if modest) body of *sleep-specific* human trials behind it, as [our magnesium guide covers](/articles/magnesium-before-bed-timing-and-dosage).

So you're paying a large premium for: a low elemental dose, animal-model cognition claims, and one small industry-sponsored human trial. For **sleep** — the thing most buyers actually want — threonate has no meaningful evidence advantage over glycinate. None.

## When threonate might still make sense

Fairness requires the other side: if your interest is *cognitive support* in aging rather than sleep, threonate is at least a hypothesis with some human signal — one of the few magnesium forms studied for cognition at all. If that's you and budget isn't a constraint, it's a defensible experiment. Track something measurable for 8–12 weeks and judge honestly.

## Bottom line

For sleep and general magnesium intake: **glycinate remains the pick** — gentler than citrate (see the [head-to-head](/articles/magnesium-glycinate-vs-citrate-for-sleep)), better price, better-matched evidence. Threonate is an interesting cognitive hypothesis wearing a sleep-aid costume, and you shouldn't pay fashion prices for the costume.
