# Facebook Native Content Strategy
# Posts designed for Facebook's algorithm: value-first, native, conversation-starting
# Post 2-3 per week. NEVER post bare links — the algorithm buries them.

## The Formula That Works:

1. **Hook** (first line — determines if people click "see more")
2. **Value body** (3-5 short paragraphs of genuine insight, ON Facebook)
3. **Question** (drives comments — the #1 ranking signal)
4. **Soft mention** (site name, no link — people Google it if interested)

### Post 1: The Melatonin Truth
**Best time: Sunday 7 PM EST (US evening browsing)**

Your melatonin bottle says 10 mg. The research says 0.5–1 mg.

That's not a typo. You're probably taking 5–10x more than you need.

Here's what the dose-finding trials actually found:
• 0.5–1 mg shifts your sleep clock effectively
• Above 1 mg, benefits plateau — but morning grogginess increases
• Your body naturally produces about 0.3 mg. You're taking 30x that amount.

Why do bottles sell 10 mg? Because US melatonin is regulated as a supplement, not a drug. There's no dose cap. Brands compete on "bigger number looks stronger" shelf logic.

The fix: buy 0.5 mg or 1 mg tablets. Take them 3-4 hours before your target bedtime (not 30 minutes before — it's a clock signal, not a sedative).

Has anyone actually tried lowering their dose? What happened?

### Post 2: The 3 A.M. Problem
**Best time: Tuesday 6 PM EST**

Falling asleep fine... then WIDE AWAKE at 3 a.m. with your mind racing?

Here's what nobody tells you: that's not one problem, it's four different problems wearing the same mask.

1. Alcohol — it sedates you early, then rebounds as it metabolizes. The "3 a.m. mystery" is often just metabolized wine.

2. Cortisol timing — if you're chronically stressed, your stress hormones can spike mid-night. That's a physiology issue, not a willpower issue.

3. Blood sugar dips — late-evening sugar or a skipped dinner causes a glucose crash that wakes you up.

4. Sleep apnea — if you wake gasping or with headaches, that's a doctor visit, not a supplement.

The fix depends on which one you have. But most people reach for melatonin — which does NOTHING for 3 a.m. wake-ups. It shifts sleep timing; it doesn't maintain sleep.

What's your 3 a.m. pattern? Do you know what triggers it?

### Post 3: The Magnesium Myth
**Best time: Thursday 8 PM EST**

If you're buying magnesium for sleep, stop buying the wrong form.

Most people grab magnesium oxide because it's cheap and everywhere. Bad news: oxide absorbs at about 4%. You're literally flushing 96 cents of every dollar.

The form I'd pick before bed: glycinate.
→ Gentle on the stomach (no midnight bathroom trips)
→ Best sleep-trial evidence of any form
→ 200–400 mg elemental, nightly, for 4 weeks

The form to avoid at bedtime: citrate.
→ Well-absorbed, yes
→ But it draws water into your intestines
→ It's literally sold as a laxative
→ A supplement whose side effect is a 6 a.m. bathroom sprint is not a sleep aid

Which form are you currently taking? (Be honest — most people have no idea.)

### Post 4: The Adaptogen Hype
**Best time: Saturday 10 AM EST**

"Adaptogen" drinks are everywhere. Sodas, sparkling waters, lattes that promise to "balance your stress response." $4 per can.

Here's what's actually inside:
→ L-theanine: typically 50 mg (the research dose is 100–200 mg, so half-dose)
→ Ashwagandha: 150 mg unstandardized (trials use 300–600 mg of KSM-66)
→ "Adaptogenic blends" at doses even optimistic studies wouldn't recognize

You're paying a 10x markup for a fraction of the effective dose, in unverified forms, with sugar.

The exceptions:
1. Theanine-forward drinks (if they list 100+ mg) — roughly a capsule in disguise
2. The ritual itself — replacing beer #3 with a "wind-down drink" is a genuine behavioral win

But the botanical claims? Marketing, not medicine.

Have you tried these? Did you feel anything beyond the placebo and the sugar?

### Post 5: The Ashwagandha Numbers
**Best time: Wednesday 7 PM EST**

Ashwagandha cut cortisol by 27.9% in the most-cited clinical trial. Let me explain why that number matters.

64 chronically stressed adults. 300 mg of standardized KSM-66 extract, twice daily, for 60 days. Not a questionnaire. Not "feeling calmer." An actual hormone measured in their blood.

A 28% drop in your primary stress hormone is significant. That's not placebo territory.

But three things determine whether YOUR ashwagandha works:
1. The extract (KSM-66 or Sensoril — not random "root powder")
2. The dose (300–600 mg/day of the standardized extract)
3. The patience (6–8 weeks minimum, not 6 days)

Most products fail on all three. Random root dust in a capsule is agricultural material, not medicine.

If you've tried ashwagandha and "it didn't work" — check: was it a named extract? Was the dose right? Did you give it two full months?

## The Rules (never break these):

1. NEVER post a bare link — Facebook's algorithm deprioritizes external links
2. Always end with a question — comments are the #1 engagement signal
3. Keep value ON Facebook — the full insight, not a teaser
4. Mention your site by name only ("I write about this at Naturally Restful") — don't link every post
5. 2-3 posts per week — never burst-post
6. Respond to every comment within 24 hours — the algorithm rewards conversation
7. When someone asks "where can I read more?" — THAT's when you drop the link in a comment (algorithm doesn't penalize links in comments)
